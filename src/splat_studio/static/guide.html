Recording guide

Splat Studio can only rebuild what the camera saw clearly. Film the object from every side, in one continuous take.

Avoid
  • Glass, mirrors, chrome and glossy paint
  • Plain white tables and blank walls
  • Turntables, because the background has to stay still
  • People, pets or curtains moving in view
  • Cuts, fast pans and zooming
  • A large open room behind the object, which turns into fog

1Pick the spot

  • Leave room to walk all the way around the object at a steady 1 to 2 m.
  • Put it on a textured surface such as a rug, newspaper or patterned cloth. COLMAP needs texture to track the camera.
  • Keep the background close and simple. Distant surroundings that are only filmed briefly become fog around the model.
  • Matte objects work best. Cover or skip reflective and transparent parts.

2Get the light right

  • Soft, even light, like an overcast day or a bright room with several lamps.
  • Avoid harsh sun and single spotlights. Hard shadows and highlights shift as you move.
  • Don't change the lighting during the take, and keep your shadow off the object.

3Set up the phone

  • Record 4K at 30 fps in landscape. Stay on the 1× lens and don't zoom, or the phone may switch cameras.
  • Lock focus and exposure: press and hold on the object until "AE/AF Lock" appears.
  • Turn off Cinematic and Action mode. HDR is converted automatically, but it's better off.

4Record

  • One continuous take with three loops, as in the diagram. End each loop where it started.
  • Walk slowly, about one step every 2 to 3 seconds. Turn your whole body at corners instead of swinging the phone.
  • Keep the whole object in frame, filling half to two-thirds of it.
  • Total length: 1½ to 4 minutes.

5Check the footage

  • Scrub through the video. Every moment should be sharp, with no jumps, flicker or people walking through.
  • After a run, Splat Studio shows at which seconds the camera tracking broke, so you know what to film again.

Once you have the video, drop it into the Studio.
